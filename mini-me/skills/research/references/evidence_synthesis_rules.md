# Evidence Synthesis Rules

Use these rules when turning retrieved papers into an answer.

## Ground the important claims

For exact claims about:

- methods
- explicit findings
- limitations
- author statements

prefer evidence grounded by `snippet_search`.

## Do not overclaim

- One paper is not field consensus.
- A small cluster of papers may still be mixed or context-specific.
- If evidence is thin, say it is thin.

## Use citations consistently

When possible, preserve enough metadata for later citation formatting:

- title
- authors
- year
- venue or journal
- DOI or URL when available

## Handle disagreement explicitly

If papers disagree:

- say they disagree
- summarize the disagreement briefly
- note whether the disagreement seems methodological, temporal, domain-specific,
  or evidence-quality related

## Recency vs importance

- prioritize recent work when the topic changes quickly
- include older seminal work when it provides necessary context

## Scope discipline

Keep the synthesis aligned to the user's actual question.

Do not widen the topic so much that the answer becomes a general survey unless
the user asked for one.
